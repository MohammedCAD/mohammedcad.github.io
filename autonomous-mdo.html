01 / Engineering concept

What is Autonomous MDO?

Autonomous MDO is a computational engineering platform for systematic exploration and improvement of mechanical designs. It brings together disciplines often addressed in separate workflows: optimization theory, parametric geometry, finite element modelling, computational decision-making, numerical reliability and engineering verification.

Its central engineering idea is to make these activities interact. A change in a design variable can alter the geometry, stiffness, stress distribution, meshing behaviour and feasibility of a proposed solution. The system coordinates these dependencies so that the next design decision can be informed by the results of previous analyses.

Where a conventional script typically follows one predefined algorithm and a fixed analysis sequence, Autonomous MDO is structured around a broader set of problem-aware numerical strategies and feedback-driven engineering evaluations.

A feedback-driven cycle: candidate assessment informs further design exploration rather than ending at a single solver execution.

The engineering challenge is not simply finding a numerical minimum. It is coordinating design exploration with physically meaningful analysis and defined acceptance criteria.

02 / Numerical optimization

Problem-adaptive optimization intelligence

Mechanical design problems do not share a universal mathematical structure. Some are smooth and differentiable; others include noisy structural responses, mixed design variables, expensive simulations, nonlinear constraints, multiple objectives or several local optima. An algorithm that is effective on one class of problem may be unsuitable for another.

Autonomous MDO integrates 14 optimization algorithms and strategies through OpenMDAO and the surrounding engineering workflow. The decision architecture is designed to match search methods to the characteristics of the problem, instead of relying on a single optimization algorithm for all design tasks.

Complementary numerical methods

The method portfolio includes SLSQP, COBYQA, COBYLA, trust-region constrained methods, IPOPT, NSGA-II/III, mixed-variable evolutionary search, differential evolution, adaptive epsilon-constraint approaches and multistart or hybrid global–local strategies. These are complementary tools rather than interchangeable solutions.

What does “best design” mean?

For a single-objective constrained problem, optimization seeks a feasible candidate with favourable objective performance. In a multi-objective problem, there may be no single design that is superior in every respect; a Pareto set instead reveals the engineering trade-offs. Structural adequacy, geometric feasibility and the applicable qualification requirements remain essential to the final decision.

The system does not treat optimizer termination as proof of a globally optimal or structurally qualified design.

03 / Computational mechanics

Structural simulation as part of the design process

Finite element analysis is integrated into the evaluation of candidate geometries. CalculiX provides structural solution capabilities, while the surrounding platform coordinates analysis preparation, mesh assessment, interpretation of numerical results and feedback to optimization.

Different structural behaviours demand different formulations and acceptance criteria. A static strength problem, for example, cannot be evaluated using the same physical quantities as a stability or vibration problem. The architecture is intended to accommodate the analysis formulation appropriate to the engineering case.

Static structural response

Loads and constraints lead to displacement, stiffness and stress responses. These quantities can become objectives, constraints or engineering acceptance measures, depending on the design problem.

Nonlinear structural behaviour

Problems involving material nonlinearity, large deformation or contact demand suitable models and solution controls. Their interpretation is distinct from a linear elastic analysis.

Structural stability and buckling

Compressive loading can introduce stability limitations. Buckling assessments therefore address failure modes not captured by stress limits alone.

Modal and dynamic characteristics

Natural frequencies and deformation modes provide information relevant to vibration-sensitive designs, with method selection governed by the engineering objective.

Adaptive FEM and mesh convergence

An important source of complexity is the connection between mesh resolution, physical idealization and solution reliability. The platform's adaptive structural analysis supports global and local refinement, mesh quality assessment, critical-region tracking and convergence evaluation for selected engineering quantities of interest.

As candidate geometry changes, finite element discretization and solver behaviour may also change. Analysis orchestration must therefore account for more than a single peak stress or a successful solver exit code.

Numerical singularities and physical interpretation

Idealized supports, concentrated loads and sharp geometric transitions may produce mesh-sensitive stress concentrations. Autonomous MDO incorporates numerical reliability assessment to investigate these effects and distinguish physically informative responses from potentially misleading numerical maxima.

Engineering assessment considers the relationship between model assumptions, local stress behaviour, mesh refinement and the quantities that matter for the design decision.

04 / Integrated computational engineering

Why the platform is technically demanding

Each design evaluation spans several interacting disciplines. A numerical optimizer proposes a configuration, geometric parameters define the physical model, a mesh represents that geometry, a structural solver computes the response, and the resulting quantities influence subsequent optimization steps.

These steps cannot be considered independently. Changing geometry can alter the mesh, numerical conditioning and feasible design space; changing the selected analysis may alter the meaning of a constraint; changing optimization strategy may alter the computational budget and required response information.

Computational orchestration

Coordinating parameter exchange, geometry generation, solver execution, result extraction and repeatable candidate evaluation across different engineering environments.

Numerical decision-making

Linking mathematical search behaviour with structural performance, feasibility, response interpretation and further evaluation needs.

Adaptive analysis

Managing mesh quality, refinement decisions and engineering quantities of interest across changing mechanical configurations.

Traceability and qualification

Separating numerical outcomes from accepted engineering conclusions and retaining an evidence-oriented basis for qualification decisions.

The sophistication of Autonomous MDO lies in the interaction between mathematical optimization, computational mechanics and autonomous engineering decisions—not merely in the number of algorithms it contains.

05 / Engineering maturity

From numerical convergence to engineering qualification

A mathematically converged optimizer does not automatically establish structural adequacy. Likewise, a completed FEM solution does not by itself establish that the discretization, physical model and acceptance criteria support an engineering decision.

Autonomous MDO treats engineering qualification as a distinct concern. The platform couples candidate performance and constraint evaluation with numerical assessment, convergence evidence and defined qualification criteria.

06 / Engineering contribution

An integrated approach to autonomous mechanical design

Autonomous MDO demonstrates the independent development of a multidisciplinary engineering software architecture combining optimization strategy selection, parametric CAD, adaptive structural simulation, numerical reliability assessment and evidence-based engineering decisions.

Its broader goal is to reduce fragmented manual iteration between specialized tools and enable systematic, technically informed exploration of mechanical design alternatives.
