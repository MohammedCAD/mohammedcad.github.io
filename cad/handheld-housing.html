CAD CASE STUDY 04 / 12 · CATIA V5 / surface design

Handheld drill housing

Wireframe construction, multi-section surfaces, trimmed patches and controlled surface transitions.

Design & modelling approach

Design objective

The handheld drill housing combines a rounded motor enclosure with a tapered grip. Its continuous silhouette is divided into an upper body, a lower body and the transition into the handle. These regions need deliberate surface boundaries to preserve ergonomic proportions while providing a coherent shell for internal components.

Surface construction workflow

In CATIA V5 Generative Shape Design, the modelling sequence starts with organised geometrical sets and construction geometry: points, reference planes, lines and splines that control the housing outline. Multi-Sections Surface establishes broad upper and lower exterior patches using defined section curves. Projection transfers reference curves onto those patches so additional detailing remains associated with the housing geometry.

Surface trimming and transitions

Split trims overlapping or unwanted portions of the generated surfaces; Offset Surface creates parallel construction geometry; Blend joins adjacent surface edges and controls transitions in curvature. Fill can close smaller bounded regions and Join groups connected patches into a coherent surface definition. The label recess is formed conceptually by an offset contour, trimmed patch and blended perimeter.

Mechanical design considerations

A production housing needs clearance for the motor, chuck and trigger mechanism; ergonomic grip diameter; parting interfaces for left and right shells; and sufficient local wall thickness around fasteners and supports.
