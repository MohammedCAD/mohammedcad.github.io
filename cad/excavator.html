CAD CASE STUDY 03 / 12 · Heavy equipment / mechanical design

Wheeled excavator

Articulated boom, chassis and mechanical linkages

Design & modelling approach

Design objective

The model depicts a compact wheeled excavator with a structural chassis, enclosed operator cab, pivoting boom, stick and bucket. Its engineering architecture combines styled exterior panels with connected mechanical members that must move relative to each other.

Cab and body surface modelling

The cab roof, windscreen enclosure, wheel guards and rear body cover use curved envelopes whose quality depends on consistent boundary curves. Wireframe geometry—splines, projected boundaries and positioned sections—provides control of the silhouette. Multi-section or boundary surfaces establish larger curved patches, while Split, Join and Blend operations are suitable for closing the body and maintaining controlled transitions between surfaces.

Boom and linkage geometry

The boom, stick and bucket are separate articulated components. Each link requires meaningful pivot axes, pin interfaces and clearance envelopes; extruded profiles and patterned mounting holes are a practical solid-modelling complement to the outer surfaces. The hydraulic cylinders connect between moving links and the frame, and their attachment positions determine the attainable articulation.

Assembly logic and verification

Mechanical constraints should preserve pin rotation at each pivot while avoiding unwanted overconstraint. A movement study would examine reach, bucket orientation, hydraulic cylinder stroke and interference between the boom, cab, chassis and wheels. The image demonstrates assembly intent but is not itself a completed kinematic or load-bearing verification.
